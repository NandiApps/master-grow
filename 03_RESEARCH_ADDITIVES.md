## PART A: PAR INTENSITY (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)

### What is PAR?
- Wavelengths 400–700nm that plants use for photosynthesis
- Measured in **PPFD: µmol/m²/s** (photons per square meter per second)
- Higher PAR = more photosynthesis, more growth, more resin

### Optimal PAR Ranges (Cannabis)

| Stage | Optimal PPFD | Growth Effect | Stress Effect |
|-------|------------|--------------|--------------|
| Seedling | 200–400 | Delicate growth | Too high = burn |
| Vegetative | 600–800 | Dense, compact growth | >900 = photoinhibition |
| Flowering | 800–1000 | Resinous, dense buds, more cannabinoids + terpenes | >1100 = stress response, secondary metabolite production |

### Key Mechanic: PAR as Stress Tool

**Normal PAR (600–900):** Optimal growth, baseline yield.  
**High PAR (1000–1200):** Photoinhibition stress → **triggers defense response** → **increases secondary metabolites** (terpenes, cannabinoids).

**BUT:** High PAR without support additive (SuperSi) causes plant damage, reduced yield.

**WITH SuperSi:** High PAR stress is channeled into terpene/cannabinoid production without plant damage.

### Terpene Production Mechanism
- Plants produce terpenes as "photo-protectants" under light stress
- Light stress activates metabolic pathways that produce volatile organic compounds (terpenes)
- Effect is **strain-dependent** but measurable
- Growers use controlled light stress + additives to increase terpene profile

## PART B: SUPER SI (MONO SILICIC ACID)

### Product Details
- **Brand examples:** Power Si, Super Si, Quantum SI
- **Cost (AUD):** ~$15–25 per 500mL bottle
- **Dosage:** 5–10 mL per 20L tank (low concentration)
- **Application:** Throughout entire cycle, every water change
- **Per 90-day cycle cost:** ~$40–60

### Mechanism
- **Strengthens cell walls** via silica deposition in plant tissues
- **Thicker stems, stronger vascular structure**
- **Protects against pests, disease, environmental stress**
- **Improves nutrient uptake** (especially secondary metabolites)
- **Fortifies plant against high-PAR stress** (photoinhibition tolerance)

### Effects on Terpenes/Cannabinoids
- **Direct:** NOT a terpene/cannabinoid booster (no direct chemical effect)
- **Indirect:** Enables plant to handle controlled stress → allows grower to push PAR higher → stress response triggers secondary metabolite production
- **Net effect:** SuperSi = "stress tolerance," allowing high-PAR strategy to work

### Game Mechanic Role
- **Protective additive** that unlocks aggressive PAR strategy
- Without SuperSi: High PAR causes plant damage, yield penalty
- With SuperSi: High PAR stress is channeled productively

## PART C: CHITOSAN (PLANT DEFENSE SIGNALING)

### Product Details
- **Source:** Derived from crustacean exoskeletons (shrimp, crab)
- **Brand examples:** Chitosan powder, foliar spray formulations
- **Cost (AUD):** ~$20–30 per 500g powder
- **Dosage:** 10–50 mL foliar spray per plant, applied weekly or bi-weekly
- **Application window:** Typically mid-vegetative through early flower
- **Per 90-day cycle cost:** ~$50–80

### Mechanism (Plant Defense Signaling)

Chitosan triggers **plant immune signaling** via:
- **MAPK cascades** (mitogen-activated protein kinase pathways)
- **Jasmonic acid** (JA) synthesis → plant defense hormone
- **Salicylic acid** (SA) synthesis → pathogen resistance
- **Ethylene** production → stress hormone

This mimics a **controlled pathogen threat**, causing the plant to redirect resources toward **secondary metabolite production** (terpenes, cannabinoids, flavonoids).

### Effects on Terpenes/Cannabinoids

**Experimental Evidence:**
- Methyl jasmonate (synthetic JA) + chitosan combination:
  - **CBDA +78.4%** (cannabinoid acid precursor)
  - **Terpenes +94.7%** (nearly doubled terpene production)
  - **Phenolics +21.2%, Flavonoids +49.5%**
- Field hemp studies (chitosan foliar spray):
  - **Total phenolics +36–69%**
  - **Antioxidant activity +12–28%**

### Game Mechanic Role
- **Primary terpene/cannabinoid booster**
- Signals plant to produce defense chemistry (which includes aromatic terpenes, cannabinoids)
- **Timing matters:** Apply at key growth stages (early flower transition) for maximum effect
- **Risk:** Overuse may stress plant excessively → diminishing returns

## PART D: OTHER BIOSTIMULANTS (NATURAL ALTERNATIVES)

### Kelp Extract
- **Cost (AUD):** ~$10–15 per 500mL
- **Effect:** Natural plant hormones, trace minerals
- **Terpene/Cannabinoid boost:** Mild (+5–10%)
- **Use case:** General plant vigor, mineral supplementation

### Triacontanol (from Alfalfa)
- **Cost (AUD):** ~$20–25 per 250mL
- **Effect:** Naturally occurring PGR, mimics plant growth hormone
- **Terpene/Cannabinoid boost:** Moderate (+10–20%)
- **Use case:** Accelerate flowering, increase metabolite production

### Jasmonic Acid (JA) / Methyl Jasmonate (MeJA)
- **Cost (AUD):** ~$30–50 per 250mL (premium additive)
- **Effect:** Synthetic plant defense hormone (same pathway as chitosan triggers)
- **Terpene/Cannabinoid boost:** Very high (+50–100%)
- **Use case:** Precision terpene production targeting
- **Caveat:** Expensive, timing-sensitive

## PART E: WHAT NOT TO USE (SYNTHETIC PGRs)

### Bad Synthetic PGRs (to avoid in this game)
- **Paclobutrazol** — Suppresses terpene/cannabinoid production
- **Daminozide** — Inhibits terpene biosynthesis enzymes
- **Chlormequat chloride** — Reduces cannabinoid potency

**Why they're bad:**
- They force growth over chemistry
- Smaller trichomes (which contain terpenes/cannabinoids)
- Lower cannabinoid concentration
- Lower terpene diversity

**Game mechanic:** These could exist as "cheap but low-quality" options; players choose quality over quantity.

## PART F: GAME META-GAME MECHANICS

### The Strategy Loop

**1. Select PAR Level**
- Low PAR (600): Safe, baseline yield, lower terpenes
- Medium PAR (800): Balanced growth + stress response
- High PAR (1000+): Aggressive; requires SuperSi to avoid damage

**2. Manage Stress with SuperSi**
- High PAR WITHOUT SuperSi → photoinhibition → plant damage → yield ↓
- High PAR WITH SuperSi → stress tolerance → plant stays healthy → terpenes ↑

**3. Trigger Chemistry with Chitosan**
- Chitosan applied weekly/bi-weekly → defense signaling → terpenes ↑↑ (78–94% boost)
- Timing matters: apply too early (waste); too late (missed window)

**4. Monitor Outcomes**
- Growth rate (cm/day) reflects PAR + nutrient balance
- Terpene profile (phenotype) determined by strain + additive strategy
- Cannabinoid potency increases with controlled stress
- Yield can increase 10–50% with optimal additive timing
- Harvest speed can accelerate with high PAR + jasmonic acid

### Possible Strain × Additive Outcomes

| Strategy | Outcome | Harvest Time | Yield | Terpenes | THC |
|----------|---------|-------------|-------|----------|-----|
| Low PAR, no additives | Baseline | Day 70 | 85g | Mild | Standard |
| Medium PAR + SuperSi | Safe boost | Day 68 | 95g | Enhanced | +5% |
| High PAR + SuperSi + Chitosan | Aggressive | Day 65 | 100g | Intense (+80%) | +15% |
| High PAR + SuperSi + MeJA | Premium | Day 63 | 105g | Ultra (+90%) | +20% |
